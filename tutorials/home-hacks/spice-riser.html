Two-Tier Countertop Spice Riser

A Batch & Build Home Hack

A stepped two-shelf riser that lifts the back row of spices where you can actually see it. It's freestanding, so there's no drilling into walls, which makes it renter-friendly.

Finished size: 16" wide, 7" deep, 5-1/2" tall at the back. The front shelf surface is 3-1/4" high and the back shelf surface is 5-1/2" high. Skill level: Beginner. Time: About 2 hours, plus drying time for the finish.

Lumber note: A "1x4" actually measures 3/4" x 3-1/2", and a "1x2" measures 3/4" x 1-1/2".

Shopping list

  • One 1x4 pine board, 4 ft long
  • One 1x2 pine board, 2 ft long
  • Wood glue
  • 1-5/8" wood screws (box of 16 or more)
  • 120- and 220-grit sandpaper
  • Finish: food-safe wipe-on oil or water-based polyurethane
  • 4 small felt furniture pads

Tools

  • Tape measure, pencil, and speed square
  • Saw: a miter box with a hand saw, or a miter saw (or ask the store to make the cuts)
  • Drill/driver with a 1/8" pilot bit and a countersink bit
  • 2 clamps
  • Safety glasses and a dust mask

Cut list

Part Qty Board Length Use
A 2 1x4 16" Front shelf and back shelf
B 2 1x4 4" Tall back legs
C 2 1x4 1-3/4" Short front legs
D 2 1x2 7" Base rails (run front to back)

The 1x4 cuts total 43-1/2", so one 4 ft board covers it with room for saw cuts.

Steps

  1. Put on safety glasses. Mark every cut with the speed square so the ends are square.
  2. Cut all the parts on the cut list. Label each one in pencil (A, B, C, D).
  3. Sand every piece with 120 grit and ease all the sharp edges.
  4. Lay out the base. Set the two D rails flat (1-1/2" face up) on a flat surface, parallel, with 16" between their outside edges, each running front to back.
  5. Attach the tall back legs. Stand each B leg on end on the BACK half of a rail. The leg's 3-1/2" face runs front to back, its outer face is flush with the rail's outer edge, and its back is flush with the back of the rail. Glue and clamp it, pre-drill up through the rail with the 1/8" bit, countersink, and drive two 1-5/8" screws into the leg.
  6. Attach the short front legs. Do the same with each C leg on the FRONT half of each rail, flush to the front and outer edges. The front and back legs now meet end to end.
  7. Attach the back shelf. Glue the tops of both B legs. Set one A board on them, flush with the outside of the legs and the back edge. Pre-drill and countersink, then drive two 1-5/8" screws down through the shelf into each leg.
  8. Attach the front shelf. Repeat with the second A board on the C legs, flush at the front and the ends.
  9. Check for wobble on a flat counter. Wipe away glue squeeze-out with a damp rag and let it dry 1 hour.
  10. Sand the whole riser with 220 grit and wipe off the dust.
  11. Apply the finish following the can's directions (usually 2 thin coats) and let it cure fully.
  12. Stick a felt pad under each end of each rail so it won't scratch the counter.

Every joint gets glue plus two 1-5/8" screws in pre-drilled, countersunk holes.
